Step 6: Data transformation to monthly ts

blk_monthly = blk_clean %>%
  mutate(YearMonth = floor_date(Date, "month")) %>%
  group_by(YearMonth) %>%
  summarise(Adj.Close = mean(Adj.Close), .groups = "drop")
blk_ts = ts(blk_monthly$Adj.Close,
             start     = c(2016, 3),
             frequency = 12)
class(blk_ts)
## [1] "ts"
  • Metric Selection: The Adjusted Close price was selected as the target variable for this 10-year longitudinal analysis because it accurately reflects the asset’s true value after accounting for corporate actions, such as dividend payouts and stock splits.
  • Aggregation: The data was aggregated from a daily to a monthly frequency to reduce microscopic market noise and high-frequency volatility, thereby enhancing the stability and reliability of the time series forecasting models.

Step 10: Exponential smoothing

  • Model Assumptions and Hypotheses:In applying exponential smoothing techniques, we construct competing hypotheses regarding the underlying structural components of the Black Rock stock price:

    • \(H_0\): The time series contains only a fundamental level with random noise, exhibiting no significant trend or seasonal components (Simple Exponential Smoothing is optimal).
    • \(H_A\): The time series possesses a definitive structural trend and/or seasonal variation (Holt’s Linear or Holt-Winters models will yield significantly lower forecast errors).

  • Stationarity Diagnostics (KPSS Test): To formally test for stationarity, we apply the Kwiatkowski-Phillips-Schmidt-Shin (KPSS) test.

    • \(H_0\): The time series is level or trend stationary.
    • \(H_A\): The time series possesses a unit root (is non-stationary).
    • Result: The calculated KPSS test statistic is \(2.1444\), which significantly exceeds the \(5\%\) critical value of \(0.463\). Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis of stationarity on the significance level of 0.05%. The data must be differenced to stabilize the mean.
